01Read this first

Five things shape this trip and all five are decided before you leave. Everything else is detail.

1 · Alpe di Siusi can only be reached by cable car in the afternoon

The access road from St. Valentin up to Compatsch is closed to private uphill traffic 09:00–17:00. No ticket buys your way past it; the 2026 parking reservation requires clearing the checkpoint by 09:00 and the barrier by 09:30. An afternoon arrival by car is refused at the gate.

So you go up by lift. Drive Ortisei → Seis/Siusi (~30 min), park free at the cable-car valley station, and ride the Seiser Alm Bahn to Compatsch — €30 return each, 15 minutes. The official access page: "Those using the cable car from Seis bypass these restrictions entirely."

2 · The last cable car down is almost certainly 18:00, not 19:00

I've now read the operator's own printed 2026 brochure. Its German and Italian columns both give:

22.05.–06.06.2026  8:00–18:00
07.06.–06.09.2026  8:00–19:00
07.09.–02.11.2026  8:00–18:00

Tuesday 8 September falls in the last band — 08:00–18:00. The brochure's English column has a typo (it prints 07/09 as both the end of one band and the start of the next); German and Italian agree the switch is on Monday 7 September.

What settles it: that switchover matches the rest of the Seiser Alm transport system. The Almbus (line 11) and the Seiser Alm Express (line 10) both withdraw their late runs after Sunday 6 September — the timetables say "verkehrt bis 06.09.2026". The whole network steps down on Monday 7 September. The website's claim of a 14 September switch fits nothing else.

Plan for 18:00. The itinerary below has you on the 17:45 descent. If you want it confirmed anyway: +39 0471 704 270 or info@seiseralmbahn.it.

3 · Lago di Braies is gated 09:00–16:00 — arrive before 09:00

The 2026 rule is Wed 1 July – Tue 15 September, 09:00–16:00, and 9 September falls inside it. Arrive inside that window and you need a pre-booked ticket: the €44 combination ticket (its €22 voucher only applies if booked a day ahead), or P3 at €25.

Arrive before 09:00 and none of it applies. Brunico is ~40 minutes away, the lake is empty, and still morning water is what gives you the reflection.

4 · Seceda's first lift is 08:30, and the morning light is backlit

Leave Bolzano at 07:15 and you'll be on the first ascent with nothing wasted.

The famous ridge faces east to ENE (bearings to the Odle/Fermeda peaks, computed from OSM coordinates: 71°–84°). In the morning the sun is directly behind the peaks — you get silhouettes and dark rock faces. The light only turns frontal and warm from about 15:00.

The morning is still the right call. Provincial weather station data at 2,477 m shows 08:00–11:00 is the sunniest, clearest window (~70% sunshine), dropping to 50–57% in the afternoon with roughly double the shower risk, and ~40% of afternoon hours at that altitude spent inside cloud. Morning buys a clear view; afternoon buys good light. A backlit view is still a view — a view inside a cloud is nothing.

5 · Bolzano ZTL — fines arrive up to a year later

Mustergasse is named verbatim inside the ZTL boundary (Delibera Giunta Comunale n. 518 del 10.10.2022), and the Bolzano ZTL is active 24 hours a day, every day with 14 automatic plate-reading gates. There is no open window. Guests of a property inside the zone are permitted luggage unloading only — never parking — and only if the property pre-registers your number plate with the Polizia Municipale. You cannot self-register; the online portal requires an Italian digital ID (SPID/CIE).

The trap: you won't know the plate until you collect the car at VCE. Message Residence Fink from the airport car park before you drive off. Fine is €83–€332 per gate crossing — in and out is two fines.

Why there's no car in Venice

You need none. The Autorimessa at Piazzale Roma is €35 per non-divisible 24-hour block and buys you zero extra metres — Piazzale Roma is where the road ends, and bus line 6 drops you there anyway from 85 m outside your door for €1.50. Three days of parking would have cost more than every bus and boat ticket of the whole trip combined.

Why Brunico for Tuesday and Wednesday

It sits right for everything that follows. Braies is ~40 minutes away; Dobbiaco, San Candido, Misurina and Cortina all lie on the same Val Pusteria axis; and Thursday's run out via Cortina to the A27 is a natural line south rather than a doubling-back. The cost is one long transfer on Tuesday evening.

The Venice access fee does not apply

The 2026 contributo di accesso ran 3 April – 26 July 2026 only, and the Comune formally closed the experiment on 27 July. For 11, 12 and 13 September there is no fee, no QR, no exemption code, nothing to register. Any site selling you a September Venice access ticket is a scam.

Two things about a 7-seater specifically

1. Your motorway tolls may be higher than the figures here. Italian Class A is a 2-axle vehicle measuring under 1.30 m at the front axle; taller is Class B, which costs meaningfully more. The €20.20 VCE→Bolzano figure is Class A, and many 7-seat MPVs sit just over the line. ⚠️ Check the plate inside the driver's door and budget up to ~35% more if you're Class B.

2. Parking Walther in Bolzano has a 2.10 m height limit. Most 7-seat MPVs clear it; a high-roof van does not. Fall back to Mondschein (2.40 m) or Castel Mareccio (no limit). Braies P4 also refuses vehicles over 2 m online.

For tolls and parking you are still a car, not a camper — camper rates (Braies €49, Tre Cime €60) apply above 2.20 m.

Two decisions this changes

Alpe di Siusi — the lift costs €180, the car park costs €30

The Seiser Alm Bahn is €30 per person — €180 for six on Tuesday. Driving up costs €30 for the whole car, whatever the headcount.

You can't drive up between 09:00 and 17:00. But the ban lifts at 17:00, and the operator's own 2026 brochure states verbatim that the online parking reservation requirement "gilt auch für Auffahrten nach 17:00 Uhr" — it also applies to ascents after 17:00. That sentence only makes sense if evening ascents are permitted, so the mechanism exists.

That version would save about €150, remove the cable-car timing entirely, and hand the photographer the golden hour to sunset at 19:41 on an empty plateau — with the descent unrestricted at any hour.

🔒 Book P2 Compatsch six days ahead — Wednesday 2 September — at seiseralm.it/parking. Worth one call to +39 0471 709600 first to confirm the 09:30 barrier deadline doesn't also bite in the evening.

Tre Cime di Lavaredo is worth it at this group size

The toll is €40 per car — €8 a head — and Brunico puts you about an hour away. The walk is genuinely easy: Rifugio Auronzo to Rifugio Lavaredo on trail 101 is completely flat, 20 minutes each way on a wide gravel track. The classic three-peaks view for 40 minutes of level walking.

A workable Wednesday: Braies 08:00–10:45 → Misurina ~1 h → toll road → Rifugio Auronzo ~12:15 → flat walk and lunch → leave 14:30 → Brunico ~16:00. Drop San Candido.

🔒 Needs a pre-booked slot with your plate at pass.auronzo.info. The barrier reads the plate, the ticket lasts 12 hours and dies the moment you exit. Capacity-limited — book early.

Group logistics

Book every dinner. A table for five or six is a different proposition, especially in Venice on a Friday or Saturday in September. Standing at a bàcaro works for two; for five it doesn't. Split into two groups for the cicchetti crawl, or book a table.

Mountain huts take no lunch bookings — arrive before 12:30 or expect to wait.

Basilica tickets are named and non-transferable. Five names spelled correctly at booking, five photo IDs on the day, one slot.

Everyone validates their own ticket, every boarding. Five people is five chances at a €64.50 fine on the water.

Sunday's airport taxi needs a van for five plus luggage. Book the night before and state the headcount.

If you'd rather not start at 07:00 — there's a cheaper way than the €44

P3, at Speckstube Eggerhof, is €25 per car, includes the transit permit, and states "arrival possible at any time of day". It's 400 m from the lake. That beats the €44 combination ticket outright unless you'll actually spend the €22 voucher on food.

Book at parking.speckstube-eggerhof.it. Note you cannot buy a parking-only reservation at the public lots — P1, P2 and P5 sell the €44 combination ticket and nothing else. P4, lakeside at 100 m, is ~€40 with a €16 voucher ⚠️ (that page still shows 2025 dates).

Whichever you pick, book ahead, not same-day — the €22 rebate inside the €44 ticket is only given on advance booking. Payment is card/online only, no cash, and you can rebook up to 3 days ahead.

Strong alternative for the afternoon: Kronplatz — lift from Riscone, 10 minutes from your door, running 09:00–17:00. LUMEN + MMM Corones on a €22 combined ticket, both 10:00–16:00. Near-zero walking for a 2,275 m panorama and two genuinely good museums. If the weather is clear this may beat San Candido.

Buses 440 and 446 together give a ~15-minute headway to Fischleintalboden and run 14 Jun – 11 Oct — cash only on board, as guest-card validity is contradictory between official sources.

Bad-weather fallback: MMM Ripa in Brunico Castle — 10:00–18:00, last admission 17:00, €14. ⚠️ It closes on Tuesdays, so Wednesday is your only shot at it. Add the Stadtgasse and the medieval gates. No driving, nothing booked, nothing at risk.

South Tyrol — what to actually order

Savoury

  • Speck Alto Adige PGI — juniper-smoked, air-cured ham. The regional signature. Order a Marende / tagliere: speck, mountain cheese, Schüttelbrot, pickles.
  • Canederli / Knödel — bread dumplings, either in clear broth or with melted butter and chives. The default hut lunch.
  • Schlutzkrapfen — half-moon ravioli of spinach and ricotta, browned butter, parmesan.
  • Gulaschsuppe — paprika beef soup. Exactly right at 2,000 m.
  • Schüttelbrot — brittle rye flatbread; Vinschgauer — dense rye rolls.
  • Graukäse — sour "grey cheese", a Slow Food Presidium. Pungent, genuinely local, not for everyone.

Sweet

  • Apfelstrudel — this is apple country; Mela Alto Adige is PGI-protected.
  • Kaiserschmarrn — shredded, caramelised pancake with cranberry compote. Usually enough for two.
  • Topfenstrudel, Buchteln, and Zelten (dense fruit-and-nut Christmas bread, sometimes year-round).

Drink

  • Lagrein — the dark, structured Bolzano red. The local answer.
  • Schiava / Vernatsch — light, cherryish red; Santa Maddalena is the Bolzano hillside version and is named after the very village whose access roads are now barriered.
  • Gewürztraminer — from Tramin, 25 km south of Bolzano. Aromatic, off-dry.

Mountain huts verified — 53-hut official dataset

Two pieces of common advice about South Tyrolean huts turn out to be wrong, and one is right.

They are not cash-only, whatever you've read

Of the 53 huts in the Alpe di Siusi tourism board's own database, 21 publish payment methods — 19 accept credit cards, 17 Bankomat/Maestro, and exactly one lists cash alone. Even Rifugio Bolzano at 2,457 m takes cards.

It is also the law: since 1 January 2023, refusing a card payment of any amount carries a €30 fine plus 4% of the transaction (art. 19-ter, D.L. 152/2021). The only statutory exemption is "objective technical impossibility" — there is no mountain-hut carve-out.

Still carry €60–100 in notes per day for small Schwaigen and dead terminals — but expect to pay by card, and know that a refusal is illegal.

They don't all shut at 17:00 — but the kitchen does

The hot-food window is the real constraint, and it's tight: across the 43 huts publishing one, service starts 11:00 (19 huts) or 11:30 (15 huts) and ends at 15:00, 15:30 or 16:00. Rifugio Firenze on Seceda is 11:30–15:00, with a reduced menu after. Baita Troier is 11:30–15:30.

But most huts stay open well past that for drinks, cake and a cold Marende, and huts with beds run a second kitchen ~18:00–19:30. Be sitting down by 14:00; treat 15:00 as the hard cutoff for a hot meal.

Rest days — and yours are exactly the risky ones

Several huts close one weekday, and your mountain days are Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday. Known closures: Radauer and Untertrompedeller — Monday. Gstatsch Schwaige — Tuesday. Rauchhütte — Wednesday. (Dibaita Thursday; Ritsch Schwaige Friday; Edelweiss, Messner Schwaige and Wild-o-Williams Saturday.) Check the specific hut before you walk to it.

Useful field sign: if the South Tyrolean flag is flying, the hut is staffed and serving.

You are ~5 days before even the first minor lift schedule step-down (Col Raiser 13 Sep, Seiser Alm Bahn 14 Sep) and about five weeks before hut closures begin. Nothing you want will be shut for the season.

Reading the signage

Schwaige, Alm and malga all mean a working dairy hut — own cheese, butter and bread, cheapest food, closes earliest. Rifugio or Schutzhütte means beds and a dinner service, usually higher up. Berggasthof is a proper mountain inn with the longest hours. Baita tells you nothing at all — check the hours.

Lago di Braies, Wednesday morning verified

Bolzano — the shape of a day

  • Piazza delle Erbe / Obstplatz — the daily produce market, ~200 m from Mustergasse. Speck, cheese, bread, fruit, and stalls doing hot food. The obvious breakfast-provisions and picnic stop.
  • The Portici / Laubengasse — the arcaded main street, where the cafés, bakeries and Weinstuben are. Everything is walkable from the flat.
  • Weinstuben — the wood-panelled taverns are the format to seek out for dinner: Knödel, Gulasch, a carafe of Vernatsch.
Closing days matter enormously here

South Tyrol keeps Austrian habits: many restaurants close Sunday and/or Monday, and provincial rules close retail on Sundays. Your arrival dinner is Monday 7 September — check your chosen place is actually open that night before you count on it, and have a second option. Museums frequently close Mondays too.

Venice — cicchetti, and how not to get fleeced

The Venetian format is the bàcaro: a small wine bar where you stand at the counter, point at cicchetti (small plates — baccalà mantecato on polenta, sarde in saor, folpetti, mozzarella in carrozza) and drink an ombra, a small glass of wine. Two or three bàcari in a row is dinner, and it is both the cheapest and the best way to eat in the city.

In season in mid-September

  • ✅ Sarde in saor — sardines with onion, pine nuts, raisins. Year-round and the definitive Venetian dish.
  • ✅ Baccalà mantecato, bigoli in salsa, fegato alla veneziana, granseola — all fine now.
  • ⚠️ Moeche (soft-shell crab) — a narrow spring and autumn window. Ask; don't assume.
  • ❌ Castraure (baby artichokes) — spring only. ❌ Frittelle — Carnival only.
  • Bussolai / esse buranei — the ring and S-shaped butter biscuits from Burano. Buy them on Burano on Saturday.

The coperto rule, precisely

Coperto is legal in Veneto — Lazio is the only region to have banned it. But under art. 18 of Regio Decreto 635/1940 the operator must make prices publicly known: if the coperto is not shown before you order, it cannot legitimately be charged. It runs €2–€6 pp. "Servizio" (10–15%) is separate; some places levy both, and a restaurant charging service and expecting a tip is double-dipping — tipping is not obligatory in Italy. You are entitled to a ricevuta fiscale.

The per-etto bill shock

Fish and steak are often priced "all'etto" — per 100 grams. A €9/etto sea bass is €45 if it weighs 500 g, and this is the single most common way a Venice dinner bill explodes. Ask for the weight and the total price before ordering. Disputes: Polizia Locale 041 2747070, or the city's 041 041 line.

Where not to eat

The restaurants directly on Piazza San Marco, the Riva degli Schiavoni, and the streets immediately around the Rialto bridge — coperto plus service plus per-etto pricing, with photo menus and touts as the tell. You only need to walk five minutes into Castello, Cannaregio or Dorsoduro to escape it entirely. Caffè Florian and Lavena on the Piazza are genuinely historic but levy a substantial orchestra surcharge when the band plays — that is legitimate and disclosed, but know it before you sit.

Marghera and Mestre

You sleep on the mainland, which is a working town where prices are a fraction of the islands. Thursday's arrival dinner and any night you get back late are best eaten locally rather than trekking back into Venice. There is a supermarket within easy reach of Via Grado for breakfast provisions — worth doing, as breakfast in Venice is expensive and unremarkable.

Practicalities

  • Meal times: Italy eats lunch 12:30–14:30 and dinner 19:30–22:00. South Tyrol eats earlier — dinner from 18:30, and kitchens can close by 21:00. Don't roll up in Bolzano at 21:30.
  • Cards: Italian merchants are legally obliged to accept card payment, and since Jan 2023 refusing a card of any amount carries a €30 + 4% fine. Mountain huts are not the exception people claim — 19 of the 21 Alpe di Siusi huts that publish payment methods take credit cards. Carry €60–100 in cash anyway for dead terminals.
  • Booking: anywhere good in Venice on a Friday or Saturday in September needs booking, often several days out. Bàcari never do.

11Mistakes to avoid

Dolomites

  1. Driving into Bolzano old town without the plate registered. 24/7, 14 gates, €83–€332 per crossing. In and out is two fines.
  2. Assuming the Bolzano ZTL "switches off" at some point. It does not.
  3. Assuming the 7-seater clears Parking Walther's 2.10 m limit — measure it before you commit to that garage.
  4. Parking on white lines in the Bolzano centro zone — residents only.
  5. 🚨 Trying to drive up to Alpe di Siusi in the afternoon. Uphill private traffic is banned 09:00–17:00 and enforced by camera at St. Valentin. There is no afternoon ticket. Your 14:00 arrival must be by cable car from Seis.
  6. 🚨 Assuming the last cable car down is 19:00. The printed 2026 brochure says 18:00. Until you've confirmed, plan for 18:00 — being stranded up there ends the day and the Brunico check-in.
  7. If you do drive up (the post-17:00 golden-hour option): the parking ticket is single entry, exiting voids it, and it cannot be refunded, changed or have its plate edited.
  8. Arriving at Seceda for 08:00. First ascent is 08:30.
  9. Planning to descend via Col Raiser — different operator, separate ticket, and your car is 500 m above where you'd land.
  10. Assuming a guest card gets you lift discounts. Neither pass covers Val Gardena or Alpe di Siusi lifts, nor the Almbus.
  11. Buying a Dolomiti Super Summer pass for Seceda — not accepted.
  12. Planning Lago di Braies off 2025 information. The 2026 window is 1 Jul – 15 Sep, 09:00–16:00 — wider and earlier.
  13. Booking the Braies ticket same-day — you forfeit the €22 voucher.
  14. Detouring to the Tre Cime toll road from Misurina on Thursday. €40 per car, a pre-booked slot required at pass.auronzo.info, and you do not have the time on a day that ends with an 18:00 car return.
  15. Underestimating September at altitude. ~9 °C max at 2,500 m, sub-zero nights possible, 40% chance of cloud immersion in the afternoon. Layers and a wind shell, especially for a dawn photoshoot.
  16. Assuming shops are open on Sunday in South Tyrol, or museums on Monday. Several mountain huts also take a weekday off — Gstatsch Schwaige closes Tuesday, Rauchhütte Wednesday.
  17. Letting Cortina overrun on Thursday. Departure at 14:00 is a hard deadline, not a suggestion — Cortina to VCE is 2h30–3h and the car is due back at 18:00.
  18. Returning the hire car without photographing the fuel gauge, mileage and all four corners.
  19. Reaching the airport without refuelling — the "not returned full" charge is worse than the local pump.

Venice

  1. Buying the €1.50 ticket and boarding a vaporetto. It is land-only; the boat version is €9.50.
  2. Not validating on every boarding, including with a multi-day pass.
  3. Driving into central Mestre — 24/7 ZTL, 8 cameras.
  4. Driving into Venice — €29–€45/day for zero benefit.
  5. Paying a rental company's ZTL "admin fee" without challenging it.
  6. Buying a September Venice "access fee" ticket. It doesn't apply. Anyone selling one is scamming you.
  7. Paying a gondolier per person. €90 per gondola for 30 min, up to 5 people. Serenades are extra and unregulated. Note your start time.
  8. Sitting on bridge steps to eat — €100–200 plus a removal order.
  9. Buying Basilica tickets from a reseller — named, non-transferable, and you can be turned away.
  10. Going to Torcello mid-day without phoning 800 845 065 twenty minutes ahead.
  11. Taking a "free launch to a Murano glass demonstration".
  12. Going to the Lido on Sat 12 Sep — Film Festival closing day.
  13. Ordering fish without asking the per-etto weight.
  14. 🚨 Leaving Marghera at 10:00 on Sunday. You have no car, it's a Sunday, and check-out clashes with the flight. Leave by 07:00 and pre-book a taxi if the buses look thin.
  15. Spending both Friday and Saturday on the islands and never seeing St Mark's, the Doge's Palace, the Frari or San Rocco.
  16. Missing the Friday/Saturday Doge's Palace late opening to 23:00 — the single best crowd-avoidance lever available on your dates, at no extra cost.
